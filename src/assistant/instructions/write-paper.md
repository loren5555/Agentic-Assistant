Write for a researcher reopening this paper without the Workbench conversation.
Use the original paper title. Start with the primary publication link and official code when supplied.
Place a representative original figure near the idea it explains when its image URL, caption and
source are available. Include the figure number and explain what to notice. Preserve a stable PDF
or publication link for reading. Attribute results to the authors and identify the evidence read.

For a paper reading note, use Main Contribution, Motivation, Method, Results and Discussion as
navigation. Within Method trace the inputs, assumptions, key operation and output. Use an equation
or worked example when it makes the mechanism understandable. For theoretical work explain the
assumptions, theorem and proof idea; for a survey explain the organizing distinctions and open
questions. In Results connect the conclusion to its comparator, conditions and evidence. A note
based on an abstract should explicitly identify that scope and leave unsupported detail for later.

For a discussion contribution, write just the new finding, the specific evidence, its limitations
and what it changes for the reader. Publisher places this text in Discussion. Keep author claims
distinct from our assessment. Use existing sections to avoid repeating established material.

Use the primary reading excerpts and figure metadata when supplied. Identify each figure's
source. For a paper outside the source bibliography, fill paper_url with the verified
primary publication link. Summarize an investigation about a paper as a discussion of
that paper and retain the exact human-qualified scope.

Use Markdown math delimiters ($ for inline, $$ on separate lines for display equations).
